A ROUTE THAT FITS YOUR LIMITS

Plan, save, and take off.

Enter N cities and rows of u,v,price. Choose at most K intermediate stops and up to C coupons. A coupon pays floor(price / 2) on one flight; no flight can use two coupons. Flights are directed, so a reverse route requires its own edge.

Choose an example, import CSV, or generate a network. Select Find cheapest route, then play the journey. The aircraft follows the arrows. Coupon flights turn gold and display the original price → discounted price. Cost and stops update when each flight arrives. Pause holds the aircraft in place; stepping or scrubbing lands at the selected city.

Green is city 1, red is city N, and visited intermediate stops are orange. Route only hides unrelated flights. Parallel flights and reverse flights are drawn separately; hover their labels to inspect flight IDs. If N = 1, you are already there and the cost is zero.

A result of −1 means no route fits the limit. Coupons can remain unused. Equal-cost routes prefer fewer flights, then fewer coupons.

CSV: use optional #cities,N on the first row, followed by u,v,price and flight rows. Without city metadata, N is inferred from the largest endpoint. Include metadata to preserve isolated cities or an empty network. Exported files can be imported again; path annotations are ignored during import.

The marked CSV keeps every original flight and adds path (x), coupon, paid_price, and step. Individual row IDs distinguish parallel tickets. Prices must be nonnegative integers.
